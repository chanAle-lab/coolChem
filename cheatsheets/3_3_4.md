# Topic 3.3.4 · Alkenes

**Specification Coverage:** Electrophilic addition reactions with HBr, H2SO4, Br2; carbocation stabilities, addition polymers

## Electrophilic Addition Reactions & Mechanisms

- **Reactivity:** C=C double bond consists of a sigma bond and an exposed pi bond of high electron density, attracting electrophiles.
- • **With Br_2:** Induced dipole on Br_2. Carbocation intermediate formed. Forms 1,2-dibromoalkane. **Bromine water test:** Orange to colourless confirms unsaturation.
- • **With HBr:** Forms bromoalkane.
- • **With conc H_2SO_4:** Forms alkyl hydrogen sulfate, which upon adding water and warming hydrolyses to an **alcohol** (regeneration of H_2SO_4 catalyst).
- **Major vs Minor Products (Markovnikov's Rule):** In unsymmetrical alkenes, the electrophile H^+ attaches to the carbon with more hydrogens, creating the **more stable carbocation intermediate** (stability: **tertiary > secondary > primary** due to positive inductive effect of alkyl groups).

## Addition Polymers

- **Formation:** Alkenes join together with no other product. pi bond breaks to link monomer units.
- **Poly(chloroethene) (PVC):** Rigid and waterproof; used for drainpipes and window frames. Addition of **plasticisers** pushes chains apart, allowing them to slide, making PVC flexible (used for electrical cable insulation and clothing).
- **Properties:** Saturated non-polar carbon backbone makes polyalkenes chemically inert and non-biodegradable.
