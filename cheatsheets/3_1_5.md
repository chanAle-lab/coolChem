# Topic 3.1.5 · Kinetics

**Specification Coverage:** Collision theory, activation energy, Maxwell-Boltzmann distributions, factors affecting reaction rate, catalysts

## Collision Theory & Maxwell-Boltzmann Distribution

- **Collision theory:** Reactions occur when reacting particles collide with kinetic energy &ge; activation energy (E_a) and with correct collision orientation.
- **Activation Energy (E_a):** The minimum energy required for colliding particles to react.
- **Maxwell-Boltzmann curve features:**
- • Starts at origin (no molecules have zero energy).
- • Peak represents **most probable energy (E_mp)**.
- • Mean energy is to the right of the peak.
- • Asymptotic: never touches x-axis at high energies (no theoretical maximum energy).
- • Total area under curve equals total number of gas molecules.

## Effect of Temperature, Concentration & Catalysts

- **Effect of higher temperature:**
- • Curve peak shifts to the right and lowers; distribution broadens.
- • Area under curve remains constant.
- • A significantly larger fraction of molecules have energy &ge; E_a.
- • Collision frequency increases slightly, but the main factor causing rapid rate increase is the greater proportion of successful collisions.
- **Effect of concentration / pressure:** More particles per unit volume -> collision frequency increases -> rate increases proportionally.
- **Catalysts:** Increase the rate of reaction by providing an alternative reaction pathway with a lower activation energy. Catalyst is unchanged at end. On Maxwell-Boltzmann, E_a line shifts to the left, meaning a greater fraction of molecules have energy &ge; E_cat.
