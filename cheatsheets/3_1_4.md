# Topic 3.1.4 · Energetics

**Specification Coverage:** Enthalpy changes, standard enthalpy of formation and combustion, calorimetry, Hess's Law, mean bond enthalpies

## Standard Enthalpy Definitions

- **Enthalpy change (deltaH):** Heat energy change measured under constant pressure conditions. Exothermic (deltaH < 0), Endothermic (deltaH > 0).
- **Standard conditions:** 100 kPa pressure and stated temperature (usually 298 K / 25°C).
- **Standard enthalpy of formation (delta_fH°):** Enthalpy change when 1 mole of a substance is formed from its constituent elements in their standard states under standard conditions. delta_fH° for any element in its standard state is ZERO.
- **Standard enthalpy of combustion (delta_cH°):** Enthalpy change when 1 mole of a substance burns completely in excess oxygen under standard conditions.

## Calorimetry & Calculations

- **Heat energy equation:** q = mcdeltaT (q in Joules, m is mass of solution in grams, c = 4.18 J g^-1 K^-1, deltaT in K or °C).
- **Molar enthalpy:** deltaH = - (q / 1000) / n (in kJ mol^-1). Always include sign (- for exothermic, + for endothermic)!
- **Sources of error:** Heat loss to surroundings, incomplete combustion, non-standard conditions, evaporation of fuel.
- **Cooling curve correction:** Plot temperature against time before, during, and after mixing. Extrapolate cooling line back to time of mixing to find true theoretical deltaT.

## Hess's Law & Mean Bond Enthalpies

- **Hess's Law:** Total enthalpy change for a chemical reaction is independent of the route taken.
- • From enthalpies of formation: **deltaH_r = SUM delta_fH(products) - SUM delta_fH(reactants)**.
- • From enthalpies of combustion: **deltaH_r = SUM delta_cH(reactants) - SUM delta_cH(products)**.
- **Mean bond enthalpy:** Enthalpy change needed to break 1 mole of a covalent bond into gaseous atoms, averaged over a wide range of different compounds.
- **Bond enthalpy formula:** **deltaH = SUM (bonds broken) - SUM (bonds formed)** (reactants - products).
- **Discrepancy reason:** Calculations using mean bond enthalpies are approximations because they use average values from multiple compounds, whereas Hess's law uses specific experimental standard enthalpy data for that exact compound in its actual physical state.
