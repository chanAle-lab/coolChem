# Topic 3.3.10 · Aromatic Chemistry (A-Level)

**Specification Coverage:** Structure and bonding of benzene, enthalpy of hydrogenation evidence, electrophilic substitution (nitration, Friedel-Crafts)

## Structure & Stability of Benzene

- **Benzene structure:** Planar hexagonal ring (C_6H_6). Each C is bonded to 2 other carbons and 1 hydrogen. The remaining p orbital on each carbon overlaps sideways to form a delocalised pi electron system above and below the ring. All carbon-carbon bond lengths are identical (0.139 nm, between single and double).
- **Thermochemical evidence for delocalisation:**
- • Enthalpy of hydrogenation of cyclohexene is -120 kJ mol^-1.
- • Theoretical cyclohexa-1,3,5-triene (Kekulé model) would be 3 * (-120) = -360 kJ mol^-1.
- • Experimental enthalpy of hydrogenation of benzene is only **-208 kJ mol^-1**.
- • Benzene is **152 kJ mol^-1 more stable** than predicted due to delocalisation energy.

## Electrophilic Substitution Mechanisms

- Benzene undergoes substitution rather than addition in order to preserve its stable aromatic pi system.
- **1. Nitration:**
- • Electrophile generation: **HNO_3 + 2H_2SO_4 -> NO_2^+ + 2HSO_4^- + H_3O^+**.
- • Electrophile is the **nitronium ion (NO_2^+)**.
- • Maintain temperature below 55°C to prevent multiple substitutions.
- • Nitrobenzene is reduced to phenylamine for azo dye manufacture.
- **2. Friedel-Crafts Acylation:**
- • Electrophile generation: **RCOCl + AlCl_3 -> RCO^+ + AlCl_4^-** (AlCl_3 is a Lewis acid catalyst).
- • Electrophile is acylium ion (RCO^+). Product is an aromatic ketone.
- • Catalyst regeneration: AlCl_4^- + H^+ -> AlCl_3 + HCl.
