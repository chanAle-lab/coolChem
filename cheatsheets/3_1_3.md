# Topic 3.1.3 · Bonding & Structure

**Specification Coverage:** Ionic, covalent, dative covalent, metallic bonding, 4 crystal structures, VSEPR shapes, electronegativity, intermolecular forces

## Types of Bonding & 4 Crystal Structures

- **Ionic bonding:** Electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions in a giant lattice (e.g. NaCl, MgO). High mp, brittle, conducts electricity only when molten or aqueous.
- **Covalent bonding:** Shared pair of electrons between non-metal atoms.
- **Dative (co-ordinate) bond:** Covalent bond where both shared electrons are donated by one atom (e.g. NH_4^+, H_3O^+, [AlCl_4]^-, CO). Shown by an arrow from donor to acceptor.
- **Metallic bonding:** Attraction between positive metal ions and delocalised sea of electrons (e.g. Mg, Na). High electrical and thermal conductivity, malleable, ductile.
- **4 Crystal types:**
- • *Giant ionic:* NaCl (each Na^+ surrounded by 6 Cl^-).
- • *Macromolecular:* Diamond (each C bonded tetrahedrally to 4 others, extremely hard, non-conductor); Graphite (hexagonal layers bonded to 3 C, delocalised electrons conduct, weak van der Waals between layers allow sliding - lubricant); Ice (open hexagonal hydrogen-bonded lattice, density of ice < liquid water).
- • *Molecular:* Iodine (I_2 molecules held by weak London dispersion forces, sublimes easily).
- • *Giant metallic:* Magnesium lattice of Mg^2+ ions with 2 delocalised electrons per atom.

## VSEPR Shapes of Molecules & Bond Angles

- **VSEPR Principle:** Electron pairs repel to maximum separation. Repulsion order: **Lone pair - lone pair > Lone pair - bond pair > Bond pair - bond pair**. Each lone pair reduces bond angle by approx 2.5°.
- • **2 pairs:** Linear (180°, e.g. BeCl_2, CO_2).
- • **3 pairs (0 lp):** Trigonal planar (120°, e.g. BF_3, AlCl_3).
- • **4 pairs (0 lp):** Tetrahedral (109.5°, e.g. CH_4, NH_4^+).
- • **4 pairs (1 lp):** Trigonal pyramidal (107°, e.g. NH_3, H_3O^+).
- • **4 pairs (2 lp):** Bent / V-shaped (104.5°, e.g. H_2O, H_2S, NH_2^-).
- • **5 pairs (0 lp):** Trigonal bipyramidal (90° and 120°, e.g. PCl_5).
- • **6 pairs (0 lp):** Octahedral (90°, e.g. SF_6, [Fe(H_2O)_6]^2+).
- • **6 pairs (2 lp):** Square planar (90°, e.g. XeF_4, [Pt(NH_3)_2Cl_2]).

## Electronegativity & Intermolecular Forces

- **Electronegativity:** The power of an atom to attract the electron density in a covalent bond towards itself. Increases across periods and decreases down groups (F > O > N > Cl).
- **Permanent dipole:** Arises when two bonded atoms have differing electronegativities (delta&chi; > 0.4). Symmetrical molecules (e.g. CCl_4, CO_2, BF_3) have polar bonds but no overall dipole because dipoles cancel out.
- **Intermolecular forces (weakest to strongest):**
- 1. *van der Waals / London dispersion:* Induced dipole-dipole interactions due to temporary uneven electron distribution. Increases with number of electrons / molecular size.
- 2. *Permanent dipole-dipole:* Electrostatic attraction between polar molecules (e.g. HCl, CH_3Cl).
- 3. *Hydrogen bonding:* Attraction between lone pair on strongly electronegative atom (N, O, F) and an electron-deficient H atom bonded to N, O, or F. Explains anomalous high bp of H_2O, HF, NH_3.
