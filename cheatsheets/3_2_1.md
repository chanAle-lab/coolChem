# Topic 3.2.1 · Periodicity

**Specification Coverage:** Classification of elements in s, p, d, f blocks; Period 3 trends in atomic radius, first IE, and melting point

## Classification & Period 3 Trends

- **Blocks:** Elements classified into s, p, d, or f block depending on the sub-shell containing the highest energy electron.
- **Atomic radius across Period 3:** Decreases from Na to Cl. Nuclear charge increases while shielding remains approximately constant; stronger electrostatic attraction pulls outer electrons closer to the nucleus.
- **First Ionisation Energy across Period 3:** General increase due to increasing nuclear charge and decreasing atomic radius.
- • *Dip at Al (Group 3):* Outer electron is in 3p subshell, which is higher in energy and partially shielded by 3s electrons.
- • *Dip at S (Group 6):* Electron pair in one 3p orbital causes spin-pair repulsion, making the electron easier to remove than from phosphorus (half-filled 3p^3).

## Melting Point Trends Across Period 3

- **Na, Mg, Al (Metallic bonding):** Melting points increase. Ionic charge increases (Na^+ < Mg^2+ < Al^3+), ionic radius decreases, and number of delocalised electrons per atom increases (1 -> 2 -> 3), leading to stronger metallic bonds.
- **Si (Giant macromolecular covalent):** Highest melting point in Period 3. Many strong tetrahedral covalent bonds throughout 3D giant lattice require enormous energy to break.
- **P4, S8, Cl2, Ar (Simple molecular):** Melting points depend strictly on the strength of **van der Waals (London dispersion) forces** between molecules, which depend on molecular size / number of electrons: **S_8 (highest) > P_4 > Cl_2 > Ar (lowest)**.
