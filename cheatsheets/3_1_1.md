# Topic 3.1.1 · Atomic Structure

**Specification Coverage:** Fundamental particles, mass number, isotopes, TOF mass spectrometry, electron configuration, ionisation energies

## Fundamental Particles & Isotopes

- **Fundamental particles:** Proton (mass 1, charge +1), Neutron (mass 1, charge 0), Electron (mass 1/1840, charge -1).
- **Atomic number (Z):** Number of protons in nucleus. Defines the chemical identity of the element.
- **Mass number (A):** Total number of protons + neutrons in nucleus.
- **Isotopes:** Atoms of the same element with the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons. Chemical properties are identical because they have the exact same electron configuration.
- **Relative atomic mass (Ar):** The weighted average mass of an atom of an element compared with 1/12th of the mass of an atom of Carbon-12.

## Time of Flight (TOF) Mass Spectrometry

- **1. Ionisation:**
- • *Electron impact:* Vaporised sample bombarded by high-energy electrons from an electron gun; knocks off one electron: **X(g) -> X^+(g) + e^-**. Used for elements and substances with low Mr; can cause fragmentation.
- • *Electrospray ionisation:* Sample dissolved in volatile solvent and injected through a fine hypodermic needle at high positive voltage; gains a proton: **X(g) + H^+ -> XH^+(g)**. Soft ionisation, no fragmentation; Mr is m/z - 1.
- **2. Acceleration:** Positive ions accelerated by an electric field so all ions have the **SAME kinetic energy** (KE = &frac12;mv^2).
- **3. Ion drift:** Ions enter a flight tube with no electric field. Velocity depends on mass (v = sqrt(2KE/m)). Lighter ions travel faster and have a shorter time of flight (t = dsqrt(m / 2KE)).
- **4. Detection:** Positive ions hit a negatively charged detector plate, gain electrons, and generate an electric current. Size of current is proportional to abundance.

## Electron Configuration & Ionisation Energies

- **Orbitals & Sub-shells:** Orbitals hold up to 2 electrons with opposite spins. Sub-shells: s (1 orbital, 2e), p (3 orbitals, 6e), d (5 orbitals, 10e).
- **Filling order:** 1s < 2s < 2p < 3s < 3p < 4s < 3d < 4p. **4s fills before 3d** and **4s electrons are lost first** during ionisation.
- **Exceptions to know:** Chromium: [Ar] 4s^1 3d^5 (half-filled d sub-shell stability); Copper: [Ar] 4s^1 3d^10 (fully-filled d sub-shell stability).
- **First Ionisation Energy (IE):** Enthalpy change when 1 mole of electrons is removed from 1 mole of gaseous atoms to form 1 mole of gaseous 1+ ions: **X(g) -> X^+(g) + e^-**.
- **General Period 3 trend:** Increases across period due to increasing nuclear charge with similar shielding.
- **Period 3 anomalies:**
- • *Mg to Al drop:* Al loses electron from 3p subshell, which is higher in energy and shielded by 3s electrons.
- • *P to S drop:* S has paired electrons in a 3p orbital; repulsion between the paired electrons makes it easier to remove.
